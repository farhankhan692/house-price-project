5. Viva & Oral Defense Q&A Preparation

Q1: Why is Least Squares an orthogonal projection onto Col(A)? ▾
Because in an overdetermined linear system Aβ ≈ y with m > n, vector y does not lie in the column space Col(A). By the projection theorem, the unique point ŷ in Col(A) that minimizes Euclidean distance ||y - ŷ||₂ is the orthogonal projection Py = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀy. The error vector e = y - ŷ is orthogonal to Col(A).
Q2: Why compare multiple vector norms (L₁, L₂, L_∞)? ▾
- L₂ Norm: Minimizes variance, guarantees unique orthogonal projection, but squares errors.
- L₁ Norm: Measures total absolute deviation, showing robust average error per house.
- L_∞ Norm (Chebyshev): Identifies the maximum catastrophic prediction error in the entire dataset, critical for risk management in financial appraisals.
Q3: What algebraic properties must the projection matrix P satisfy? ▾
1. Idempotency: P² = P (projecting an already projected vector changes nothing).
2. Symmetry: Pᵀ = P (projection is self-adjoint).
3. Residual Orthogonality: (I - P) is the projection onto the orthogonal complement Col(A)ᗮ = Nul(Aᵀ). Thus Aᵀ(I - P)y = Aᵀe = 0.
Q4: Why use QR decomposition in addition to Normal Equations? ▾
Forming AᵀA squares the condition number: κ(AᵀA) = (κ(A))². In near-collinear problems, this leads to floating-point instability. QR decomposition via Gram-Schmidt solves Rβ = Qᵀy with condition number κ(A), preserving double numerical precision.
